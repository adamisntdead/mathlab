# Research handoff

## 1. Primary target
State the exact theorem/problem/conjecture you want the lab to investigate.

## 2. Definitions and conventions
Give precise definitions, normalization conventions, domains, notation, and any nonstandard terminology.

## 3. Established results
List results you regard as proved. Link or attach proofs/papers where useful. Distinguish published results from your own unrefereed work.

## 4. Current frontier
What are the most promising approaches, reductions, candidate lemmas, or constructions?

## 5. Computational evidence
Record exact computations, scripts, parameter ranges, numerical patterns, recurrences, valuation data, etc. State clearly where evidence ends.

## 6. Known dead ends / failed attempts
For each failed route, record *why* it failed. This is valuable negative knowledge and prevents repeated rediscovery.

## 7. Open bottlenecks
List the concrete things whose resolution would materially move the project forward.

## 8. Useful references
Papers, books, repositories, or named theorems that agents should inspect.

## 9. Existing code and data
Describe files placed in `code/`, `data/`, or `references/`.

## 10. Research preferences
Optional: e.g. favor exact computation first; search for counterexamples aggressively; formalize only after an informal proof stabilizes; preserve multiple approaches.
